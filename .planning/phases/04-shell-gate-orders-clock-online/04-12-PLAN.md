<objective>
Teach the browser harness to establish a session, point it at the five surfaces this phase
renders, and add the six geometry, reflow and focus assertions the UI-SPEC's section C defines.

Purpose: this is the largest test-infrastructure task in the phase. `scripts/check-wcag.mjs`
scans two surfaces today and has **no cookie handling at all**, and three of the five surfaces
require a session. C1, C3, C6, C7 and C8 all depend on that one new capability. Until it exists,
the phase's most important rendered claims — target size, the 130 px control, one-axis scrolling,
and focus moving on every transition — have no automated proof at all.

Output: one extended script, run as the last two steps of the one gate.

**Where a failure found here gets fixed.** This plan builds the assertions; it does not repair the
surfaces they fail on. A target under 44 px, a clock control that is not exactly 130 × 130, a
two-dimensional scroll, a missing heading or a focus move that does not land is a finding against
the plan that owns that surface — 04-07 for the gate, 04-08 for the order list and the conflict card,
04-09 for order detail and the clock, 04-10 for the time surface, 04-11 for the switcher, the header
and the focus effect. Record the finding, name the owning plan, and surface it to the orchestrator
rather than adjusting the assertion or patching the component from inside the harness plan. These
surfaces were written one to three waves earlier — the gate, the order list and the time surface in
wave 4, order detail in wave 5, the switcher in wave 6 — which is exactly why the routing is stated
rather than assumed.
</objective>

**`/?s=limits` needs a session, and that is not a mistake in this list.** Plan 04-11's switcher
renders the gate at **every** value of `s` when there is no session, because the gate is not an `s`
value — it is what any surface shows when `GET /api/session` returns 401 (locked decision D-03).
Scanning `/?s=limits` before the mint would therefore scan the gate a second time and never scan
Limits at all, while every count-based assertion still passed. Limits is a shipped Phase 1 surface
whose whole rendering path changes when the switcher lands, so this is the one scan that would catch
a regression there.

**The accepted cost, recorded rather than left implicit.** With no session, `/?s=limits` and the
ribbon's own *Read the full preview limits* link both resolve to the gate: a control whose label
names a destination it does not reach until a persona is chosen. That follows from D-03 and is not
reopened here — it is stated in the same shape as D-04's recorded cost in 04-CONTEXT.md, so a later
reader meets it as a decision rather than as a defect.

**Where a failure found here goes.** This plan owns the harness, not the surfaces. A defect this
scan surfaces is repaired in the wave-4 or wave-5 plan that owns the surface — 04-07 for the gate,
04-08 for the order list, 04-09 for order detail and the clock, 04-10 for the time surface, and
04-11 for the switcher, the header and the focus effect — and
never patched in `check-wcag.mjs` to make the scan pass. Three surfaces' worth of findings can
arrive here at once, two waves after the code was written, which is the known cost of a browser
assertion that cannot run until the switcher wires the surfaces together; naming the routing is
what keeps that cost from turning into a harness that asserts less than it claims.
